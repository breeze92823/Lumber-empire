## Current state: the chain runs, in a room of six
The player's lumber yard, recreated from reference screenshots, with a HUD and the core rules (`game.js`, `GAME` in config). The chain: chop a tree > logs land on the ground > walk over them to carry them > the LOGS IN ring tips them on the pile > Debarker > conveyor > Sawmill > Kiln > Bundler > (from Rebirth 1) Workshop > pallet > carry to a truck's SELL ring, which pays at once, or the Truck Conveyer takes it straight to the trucks. Workers do all of that by themselves. Nothing is owned at the start: the Debarker is a black silhouette behind a $10 sign and BUY ring, and until it is bought the logs go straight to the truck's SELL ring (the LOGS IN ring only appears once it is bought). A machine only needs the machine before it: with no conveyor between two owned machines, workers (or the player) carry the goods from the first one's pallet onto the next one's belt, which is slower. Cash buys the Debarker (Debarker Turbo is offered only once the Sawmill and Kiln are in, each with its conveyor; it swaps the Debarker for a bigger yellow machine (`buildTurbo` in sawmill.js: hazard gantry with spinning drums, control box with a 2X screen, tall body with hopper and flue) with its own particles in `yard/index.js` `turboFx`, a purchase pop and a shudder while it works), then the upgrade boards' rows, machines, conveyors, More Forest, Truck Bay 2, Debarker Turbo and, from Rebirth 1, the Conveyor to the Workshop, Tables, Beds, the Truck Conveyer, x2 Conveyer Speed and the Second Floor. Truck Bay 3 needs Rebirth 2 and Bay 4 Rebirth 5: each bay holds a padlocked silhouette truck (`YARD.locked`, `sale`) until its sign can stand, which also needs the bay before it owned. A rebirth (`game.rebirth`) takes the wallet, the whole plot and every upgrade level, keeps the crew, puts the player back at spawn, doubles every sale, gives a crate and +1 max worker, and, from then on, offers the Workshop ($50,000) to buy, then its Conveyor ($40,000). Everything meant to sell for Gems is in `SHOP` (config): `gems` is its future price, unused, and `money` is what it costs until Gems exist. `shop.js` runs the Hacker and OP Truck rings and the HUD's Auto Chop, x2 Money and Rush Hour, which multiply sales through `shop.cash` and survive a rebirth; the OP Truck is Truck Level 4, past the Depot's `max`. The Hire packs, Gifts' open-all and the Rebirth skip live in their windows. Still placeholders: the train, TRAIN ring and crates. Each upper floor (`YARD.floors`, `yard/floors.js`) repeats the whole line as `id@floor` sales at `priceMult` prices: a Wood Lifter (`yard/lifters.js`, three bought levels) carries that floor's share of the ground pile up (logs are shared by count, and the ground line leaves a cage load for a floor that is due one), step lifters at the end of each machine's belt (bought on the Second Floor, two levels) move items to the floor whose next machine has least to do or, if this line stops there, to one that can carry on, and a Truck Lifter drops finished items onto the shared Truck Conveyer; workers also climb the stairs to haul a floor's pallets. The Bay Conveyers (`yard/feeds.js`, `GAME.line.feeds`) load the trucks the finished goods aren't reaching: Bay 2 from the Debarker ($10,000, Rebirth 2), Bay 3 from the Sawmill ($15,000, Rebirth 3), Bay 4 from the Kiln ($400,000, Rebirth 5), ground line only. Each needs its conveyor (its sign takes that conveyor's spot), the Truck Conveyer and its bay; Bay 2's also needs Truck Bay 3, because two bays keep up with the line and have no idle truck to load. A kicker sends an item off only once the next machine has `backlog` to get through and `sky.spare` says the truck is spare (no finished item waiting for a truck, and another truck standing with room), and books room on the truck for it, so wood is never left on a belt for a truck that has gone and the finished goods lose nothing (a fixed-step run gave 100 chairs in 5 minutes with and without). A gantry crane at the head of each belt lifts items over the Truck Conveyer onto the truck (`trucks.sell` with no `from`). A rebirth removes them, x2 Conveyer Speed speeds up belt and crane. Prices pay back in about 15 minutes at the rebirth that opens them; for testing, step `game.update` in a loop, since a hidden or covered pane throttles rendering and with it the simulation. The Train Loader (Rebirth 2) branches off the Truck Conveyer, a padlocked silhouette of its own belt until it is bought: bought, the train stands at the chute and pays its whole load x2 as it leaves. Floor prices, the +2 max workers per floor, the lifter numbers and the train's pay are guesses. See Multiplayer below for saves and the other five yards. The old boat game's code is not in the tree; it is in git `HEAD` (`git show HEAD:src/<file>`) apart from uncommitted edits made before the cleanup, so check there before rewriting something generic (windows shell, net, audio, eggs, pets). `particles.js` was restored from there.

Three.js (vanilla, no React) + Vite + Rapier (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`, wasm inlined; `main.js` awaits its init at top level, hence `build.target: 'es2022'`). Everything is procedural (canvas textures); the only imported model is the Bloxity base rig `public/avatars/player.glb`. Font: Fredoka (loaded in `index.html`).

- `npm run dev` / `npm run build`. `VITE_DEV_MODE=true` (see `.env.example`) skips the Bloxity SDK/CDN. Deploy: `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` uploads `dist/` to Bloxity Hosting (main = prod, dev = dev channel).
- All tunables are in `src/config.js` (the rest of `src/` holds no theme values). It now has: identity (`GAME_SLUG`, the registered `lumber-empire`, `GAME_TITLE`, `DEV_MODE`, `LEADERBOARDS`), the network (`SERVER_URL`, dev only, `NET`), where the yards stand (`PLOTS`), the whole yard (`YARD`: layout, sign text, colours, workers, train, particles), `SPAWN`, `SPAWN_FACING`, `HUD`, `WINDOWS`, `MODES`, `PHYSICS`, `PLAYER_DIMS`, the look (`RENDER`, `FOG`, `TIME_OF_DAY`, `LIGHT`, `CHARACTER_COLORS`, `MATERIAL_PBR`, `GRAPHICS_PRESETS`, `BLOOM`, `NEON`, `graphicsPreset`), `TOUCH`, `CAMERA`, `SETTINGS`, and the rig (`RIG*`, `PROPORTIONS`, `ANIM`).
- All Bloxity SDK calls go through `src/bloxity.js` and must never throw (SDK may be blocked). It handles init/loading steps, auth (user/guest), settings (`graphics_quality`, `camera_sensitivity`, `show_fps`, `master_volume`; `fullscreen` is the portal's own, and `music_volume` waits for the game to have music, since listening puts a slider in the menu), avatar + proportion change events, the portal `respawn_request` event, the avatar customizer, and token for the server (`getAuthToken`), chat (`onChat`), emotes (`onEmote`, `fetchEmotes`), custom characters (`getCharacterFor`), the room toasts (`playerJoined`, `playerInRoom`), the trophy boards (`define`), friends (`refreshFriends`), ads (`onAdBreak`, `showRewarded`, `showPanelAds`) and the matchmaker helpers `net.js` uses (`resolveServerEndpoint`, `locateRoom`, `updateRoom`).
- Ads and friends: an ad break (`onAdBreak`, main.js) stops `frame()` and mutes the master bus, and the room stays joined. FREE REWARDS (`ADS` in config, `shop.js`) only shows while `ads.isAvailable()`: a watched ad's token goes to the server (`claimAdReward`), which asks Boxity, and a yes gives x2 Money. Windows show `panel` banners from `ADS.panelMinWidth` up. Friend Boost is real: `HUD.friendBoost` % on every sale per Bloxity friend in the room (`countFriends` in main.js, `hud.friendCash`); the game draws no invite button. Emotes: `avatar/emotes.js` reads the catalogue, `animator.playEmote` lays the clip over the pose (any named bone, `Arm*_Offset` too) and moving ends it; the roster's `emote` tells the others. No death screen: nobody dies (`BLOXITY_CODE_METADATA.md`). Party seating and friends' rooms are the matchmaker's, so `party.enable` and `play_with_friends` are not used.

## Files (`src/`)
- `main.js`: boot order = Bloxity SDK -> renderer -> Rapier -> clouds, lighting, ground + floor collider, yard, HUD, player body -> avatar (async, swaps in when ready) -> loop (physics, interpolate player, avatar animator, camera, yard, HUD, particles, lighting, bloom render). `__game` (dev only) exposes `player`, `scene`, `renderer`, `camera`, `postfx`, `yard` (`yard.sendTrain()`), `hud`, `windows` (`windows.rebirth.open()`), `lighting`, `bodies`, `toggleMode`, `teleport(x, y, z, facing)` (facing `Math.PI` = north, -Z).
- **Player**: `player.js` kinematic capsule + Rapier `KinematicCharacterController` (autostep, slopes, snap-to-ground, pushes dynamic bodies), own gravity/jump (coyote time + buffer). Feet position is `player.position`; `player.renderPosition` is interpolated for drawing. Falling below `PHYSICS.killY` respawns. `camera.js` third-person chase cam (boom, wheel zoom, wall clearance via `segmentClear`, speed FOV) and first person at the inner zoom end; `modes.js` `character` (avatar visible) / `player` (first person, avatar hidden), V toggles. `input.js` keyboard / mouse / pointer lock into `inputState`; `touch.js` + `touch.css` touch controls.
- `avatar/`: `character.js` (player.glb or procedural fallback), `loader.js` (Bloxity cosmetics + proportions from the CDN, `cdn.js`), `rig.js` + `animator.js` (procedural idle/run/airborne/landing; tunables in `ANIM`).
- **Physics**: `physics.js` Rapier world, fixed 60 Hz step with an accumulator; dynamic meshes bound via `bindMesh`/`addDynamic` are interpolated between ticks. Static geometry uses `addFixed` / `addFixedBoxFor`. `segmentClear` is the camera's ray against static colliders only.
- **Ground**: `world.js` `buildGround(scene, contacts)` lays the lawn, road, rail bed and concrete edge to edge with no overlaps, so nothing z-fights at distance; the concrete is one canvas with soft contact shade under the props (`yardContacts()`). `landColliders()` is one floor sized to the yard: a cuboid hundreds of metres wide lets the character sink as it moves off-centre. `createWorld` is the clouds.
- **Yard** (`yard/`, wired in `index.js`; coordinates: origin = where the reference character stands, north = boards, east = forest, west = railroad, south = Leaderboard): `kit.js` model shorthands (`box`, `bar`, `picture`, `place`, `silhouette`), `paint.js` every sign, ring and icon canvas, `perimeter.js` instanced fence + railroad + road, and each gate's drive and timber crossing, `forest.js` instanced pines, the crop's up > falling > gone > growing life (`crop`, trees inside the fence) and the logs on the ground, `boards.js` all signs and the price buttons, `vehicles.js` the bays' trucks + train, `rigModels.js` the four Truck Level trucks and the OP truck (tractor + trailer built from extruded profiles, one vertex-coloured mesh per axle, a sprung cab; a template is shared by every rig), `rig.js` one truck on the road: routes are centre lines sampled in the nose's direction, the axles sit on the line (backing up = s runs down), plus speed from the bends, gears, cab pitch and roll, steering, lamps, smoke, and it stops for the player, a truck ahead or a train at the crossing, `rigPortrait.js` the Depot's pictures, `sawmill.js` the machine models (silhouette until bought), the conveyors (absent until bought; their sign only stands once the machine they lead to is owned), pallets and the floating WORKERS / OUTPUT signs, `line.js` the belts: single file along `GAME.line.path`, distances in metres from its head, a machine's OUTPUT sign sends items NEXT or to its PALLET, and past the last machine NEXT (shown as TRUCK) is the Truck Conveyer (`GAME.line.sky`), which loads the bays in turn: items are booked onto the truck being filled, then the next bay along, `floors.js` the upper decks, posts, rails and stairs, `lifters.js` the Wood Lifters, `feeds.js` the Bay Conveyers (kicker, cleated incline, gallery on trestles, gantry crane; the belt surface scrolls by its texture offset), `items.js` everything that moves (one instanced mesh per product, re-listed each frame between `items.begin()` and `items.end()`, plus hops and wood chips), `stations.js` Workers stall, Trucks kiosk, Hacker, floor rings, alerts, locks, `workers.js` the crew's job picking: every `haulEvery`-th worker hauls pallets first, the rest gather logs, fell, then haul; they cross the line round its north end (`GAME.crew.passage`). A machine's WORKERS sign lets the crew touch its pallet; the Debarker's also rules chopping and logs. Locked items are real models passed through `silhouette()`: black with a grey rim, and any material that sets `userData.silhouette` stays grey. Materials are cached by colour and bake merges by look, so a material that needs that flag must have its own colour (see `guardRail`). `buildYard` builds a yard into any group: the player's at the origin, and the five neighbours (see Multiplayer).
- **Rules**: `game.js` is the player's side: axe, hands, floor rings, purchases (`YARD.saleSigns`: `needs` = what must be owned first, `later` = hidden until then, `also` = one more or a list that must be owned but leaves the sign up) and the haunting (`GAME.haunt`). `stats.js` holds the upgrade levels: a row in `YARD` names a `GAME.upgrades` entry by `id` and `stats.js` writes its `sub`, `price` and `level`; read current values from `stats`. The boards repaint through `boards.repaint()`, and price tags turn green when affordable (`boards.refreshPrices`). Conveyors are solid but low enough to jump.
- **HUD**: `hud.js` + `hud.css` + `hudIcons.js` (inline SVG). Sized in reference pixels times `--u`. Live: the train timer (it sends the train at zero), the wallet (`hud.earn` also shows the gain), the crate slot, the Gifts alert, the carried count, the first-steps hint (`GAME.hints`) and short notices (`hud.notify`).
- **Windows** (`windows/`, wired in `index.js`): `shell.js` the shared frame (one open at a time, a blur veil under the HUD, Esc or a click on the world closes it, fits below Boxity's toolbar via `setWindowReserved`), `gifts.js`, `rebirth.js`, `collection.js` (Index), `hire.js`, `depot.js` (Truck Depot), `art.js` their SVGs, `windows.css` everything measured off the reference (design px times `--u`, set per window by `--w` / `--h`). The HUD tiles open Gifts, Rebirth and Index; the HIRE and UPGRADE rings open Hire Workers and Truck Depot on the way in and close them on the way out. An open window hides the Shop and tile grid, as in the reference. The HUD owns the money (`hud.money`, `hud.addMoney`) and the crate slot (`hud.addCrates`). The depot's two rows are ordinary upgrades (`GAME.upgrades.truckLevel`, `delivery`, bought through `game.upgrade`): `vehicles.js` reads `stats.delivery` plus `GAME.trucks.drive` each way for the time a bay stands empty and `GAME.trucks.levels[stats.truckLevel]` for the load size, full load bonus and `rig` model. A full truck leaves by its gate and goes north up the east lane; its replacement comes south down the west lane in time to arrive when the trip is up, stops past the gate and reverses in, so two trucks of one bay can be on the road at once. A bought Truck Level swaps a parked truck on the spot (`trucks.refit`); one on the road is replaced when the next comes in. Each incoming truck rolls `WINDOWS.depot.lucky`: a lucky one changes colour, shows its `x$N`, and everything on it pays N. The Depot's picture is the truck the next level buys.
- **Audio** (`audio/`): `core.js` + `synth.js` restored from git `HEAD`, `sfx.js` the board button one-shots (all synthesised). Start it with `installAudio()`; nothing plays before the first gesture. The rest of the old audio (`engine.js`, `ambience.js`, the other one-shots) is still in `HEAD`.
- **Sky and light**: `lighting.js` sky shader, stars, PMREM environment, shadowed sun following the player, fog, day/night blend (N). `addLamp` and `setMoods(sky)` are unused hooks. `postfx.js` bloom (EffectComposer: HDR half-float render -> UnrealBloomPass -> OutputPass), on for the High/Ultra presets only (`BLOOM`; threshold 2.0 so sunlit surfaces never bloom). `NeutralToneMapping` keeps the flat toy colours. The day sun and ambient are solved from reference pixels (lit concrete about 201,198,192, shadow about 72,96,140), so retune them together.
- **Materials and textures**: `textures.js` canvas painters (cracked sand, water ripples, grass/tiles/crates/planks, outlined text, soft dots, sparkles, ...) and `toTexture` / `mulberry32`. `propkit.js` shared prop helpers: `std`, `glow`, `neon(color, k)` (unlit, HDR-bright with bloom; switched by `setNeonBloom`), `put`, `solidBox` / `solidPole` colliders, `label` (outlined text sprite, repainted once Fredoka loads). `bake.js` merges a Group's static meshes to one mesh per material (draw-call saver).
- Controls: WASD/arrows, Shift sprint, Space jump, mouse look (click grabs the cursor = pointer lock, Esc releases it, then Esc opens the portal menu), hold left click or F / E beside a tree to chop (touch: the CHOP button, shown only beside a tree), click or tap a price or machine sign to buy or switch it (with the cursor locked the click aims through the screen centre), wheel zoom, V mode, N day/night, R respawn. Never bind P (CrazyGames: Boxity's pause menu).
- Graphics: `graphicsPreset(name)` in config resolves the preset; on coarse pointers the default is Medium and every preset is capped by `MOBILE_GRAPHICS_CAP` (DPR 1.5, 1024 shadow map, no bloom), because the Bloxity SDK sends `High` itself.
- Boxity layout (required to publish, docs.bloxity.io Platform UI rules): Boxity draws the menu / pause menu, chat, emotes, avatar editor, invite, leaderboard and sign-in itself in a toolbar in the top-left corner with the chat feed under it, so the game never draws those buttons and keeps any HUD out of those rectangles (`bloxity.onReservedAreas`).

## Multiplayer
A room is a world of six yards, one player each (backend CLAUDE.md for the server's side). `npm start` in the backend and a Vite dev server here is enough to play: in dev the game connects to `ws://localhost:2567` (`VITE_SERVER_URL_DEV`), and built games only ever go through the matchmaker. Two tabs are two players: dev ids are per tab (`sessionStorage`).
- **The layout** (`PLOTS`, `yard/plots.js`): two columns of three either side of the road, the west column turned half way round about the road's middle, so it is the same yard, not a mirror image, and they share the road. The player's own yard always stands at the origin, so every coordinate in the game means what it always did, and the others stand relative to it. Which slot is ours is only known once the server answers, so the five neighbours are built at boot and moved into place then: each is one group plus one fixed body (`createHolder`/`setHolder` in `physics.js`) all its colliders hang from.
- **A neighbour's yard** is `buildYard(group, lighting, { book, remote: true, view })`: the same code with its own `createBook()` numbers (`stats.js`), minus the Leaderboard (its fence gap is closed), ambient particles, tree tags, lamps and sounds, and with a crew of puppets (`buildPuppets`). `remoteTextures` in `paint.js` makes all five share one set of canvases; a painter whose picture differs per yard passes `own` to `liveTexture`. It answers no input, and rings only look at the player's own coordinates, so a visitor can walk round it and change nothing. The server keeps that true whatever a client sends.
- **What a yard owns** is data (`yard/plot.js`, `createPlot`): `buy`, `wipe`, `load`, `follow`, `state`, and `EFFECTS`, what each sale does. `game.js` keeps the money, the hands and the axe. A purchase, a loaded save and a neighbour's yard all go through this, so a new sale needs its effect in one place.
- **Live sync** (`yard/sync.js`): the owner is the truth. `plot` goes through the roster when it changes; `yardFast` (~5 Hz, the crew and falling trees) and `yardSlow` (~1 Hz, trees, logs, belts, piles, pallets, trucks, train) are relayed unread as nested number arrays, each subsystem's `snapshot()` and `restore()`. A viewer runs the same machinery, so belts and trucks move between frames and a frame only sets them right (`settle` in `line.js` nudges an item within 2 m rather than snapping it). Viewers run no crew AI and no tree regrowth. A frame is untrusted: anything reading one is wrapped, and anything drawn from one must cope with nonsense. Items travel as one number (`itemCode`), which loses `mult`: a viewer's copy only ever draws them.
- **Other players** (`remotePlayers.js`): the Bloxity look travels as ids (`{ equipped, proportions }`), and each client builds the skin URL itself (`composedSkinUrl`, as the docs say for other players). `avatar/look.js` builds a dressed character for the player and the others alike, custom characters included (`getCharacterFor`). Poses are in the sender's own yard's coordinates and go through `plots.map`. Chat lines show over heads (`bubble.js`); Boxity draws the chat itself. Guests have no `uid`, so no bubble shows for them.
- **Saves** (`net.js`, `save` in `main.js`): the server's doc is the only save. `snapshot()` = wallet, career, `game.snapshot()`, `windows.snapshot()`; `hydrate(doc)` puts all of it back. Anything new a player keeps goes in both, and in the backend's `sanitize.ts` and `PlayerDoc`. Leaving the page leaves the room on purpose, which frees the seat at once; a dropped connection keeps its seat and yard for 20 s.
- **The Leaderboard** stands in the player's own yard only (`yard/leaders.js` holds the server's lists; offline it shows just You). The same three boards are on Boxity's trophy button (`LEADERBOARDS`, backend `PLATFORM_BOARDS`).
- **Testing**: a background tab pauses `requestAnimationFrame`, so to run two players at once drive the hidden one with `setInterval(__game.frame, 33)`. `__game` also has `plots`, `remotes`, `net`, `save`. A hidden tab boots with a 0-size canvas, so the Depot's pictures stay blank there. Other tabs doing that on one machine wreck the frame rate of the one you are measuring: stop their pumps first.
- **Known limits**: items inside lifters and Bay Conveyers are local between frames; a truck on the road can run a few seconds off the owner's; road and rails are shared, so a neighbour's truck or train passes through another's; a viewer's perks and boosts don't change what it draws.

## Mobile & responsive (required for every change)
The game must play on phones and tablets (portrait and landscape) as well as desktop. Treat a feature as unfinished until it works on touch and on a small screen.
- **Input**: every action needs a touch path, not just a key. Use Pointer Events (`pointerdown/move/up`, check `e.pointerType`), never mouse-only events. Layout (implemented in `src/touch.js`): left-thumb virtual joystick -> `inputState.move`, right-side drag -> `inputState.look`, on-screen buttons for jump/sprint/mode/etc., pinch -> `inputState.zoom`. Everything still feeds `inputState` in `src/input.js` so game code stays input-agnostic. Pointer lock does not exist on touch; never rely on it.
- **Detection**: decide touch UI with `matchMedia('(pointer: coarse)')` / `navigator.maxTouchPoints`, not the user agent. Show touch controls only on coarse pointers; a device can have both.
- **Layout/UI**: HUD and menus use relative units (`%`, `vw/vh`, `dvh`, `clamp()`) and flexbox/grid, no fixed pixel layouts. Tap targets >= 44px. Respect notches with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` (viewport already has `viewport-fit=cover`). Text readable at 360px width. No hover-only interactions. Add `touch-action: none` on the canvas and game controls so the browser does not scroll/zoom.
- **Resize/orientation**: anything sized to the screen (camera aspect, renderer, HUD) must react to `resize` (covers orientation changes). Use `visualViewport` where the on-screen keyboard or browser bars matter.
- **Performance**: mobile GPUs are weak. Respect the `graphics_quality` presets in `src/config.js` (cap DPR, shadow map size, draw distance); default to a lower preset on coarse-pointer devices. Avoid per-frame allocations; keep draw calls and texture sizes low.
- **Testing**: check every UI/input change at a phone viewport (e.g. 375x812 and landscape 812x375) with touch emulation, plus desktop, before calling it done.
